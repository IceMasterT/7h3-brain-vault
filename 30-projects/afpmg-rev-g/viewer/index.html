Why G-A won the comparison

  • The bottleneck in Rev 3.1 is copper width at the inner radius. Moving the ring out to r 75 mm and thickening the stator gives 2.6× the copper area per leg.
  • G-C (two stators, three rotors) adds two more rotor faces spinning in narrow gaps. In this model that windage cancels its copper gain: 53.8 W against 52.2 W.
  • G-B (16 poles) has shorter pole pitch, so more field leaks between poles and strand eddy loss rises with frequency: 54.8 W.
  • Once copper is fixed, the floor is set by windage (11.7 W) and the converter (13.6 W). Further gains must come from those two.

Sweep: 59,040 geometries across 1–3 stators, 12/9, 16/12 and 20/15, then an integer-turn refinement. Same converter, bearing and lead assumptions for every candidate.

G-A1 at 3 kW: 12.2 N·m shaft torque, 63.8 A rms phase current. Stretch converter means 5 devices per switch at 0.25 mΩ, 16 kHz, 0.25 mΩ inductors.

How sure is this

  • 98% is reached only with the stretch converter, by 1.05 W at 2.4 kW and 0.47 W at 3 kW. That margin is smaller than the model's own uncertainty.
  • Windage is the least certain term. If it is 50% higher than the Daily-Nece scaling predicts, loss rises 5.9 W and the design lands at 97.8%.
  • The field model is 2D Fourier with a fringing factor. The thicker gap and stator make 3D FEA more important here than it was for Rev 3.1.
  • Rev 3.1 text gives 2.05 mWb flux per pole. Its own 0.742 T face field gives 1.23 mWb, which matches its 1.25 T back-iron claim. Treat 2.05 as a source error.
